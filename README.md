# Radiology-medical

A descriptive reference for radiology scans, covering both X-Ray and MRI
modalities. Every description below explains what the scan is, what it shows,
how it works, why it's done, and what to expect.

---

## X-Ray

X-ray imaging (radiography) uses focused beams of ionizing radiation to
produce two-dimensional images of the inside of the body. Dense structures
such as bone block more of the X-ray beam and appear white, while air-filled
areas appear dark.

### Chest X-Ray

- **What it is:** A chest radiograph that produces an image of the heart,
  lungs, airways, blood vessels, and the bones of the spine and chest wall.
- **What it shows:** Lungs (pneumonia, fluid, masses, collapsed lung); heart
  size and shape (enlarged heart, heart failure); ribs and spine
  (fractures); airway position.
- **How it works:** An X-ray beam is passed through the chest onto a detector.
  Bones and soft tissue appear in shades of gray; air appears dark.
- **Why it's done:** Commonly used to investigate cough, chest pain, shortness
  of breath, and to check placement of devices (tubes, pacemakers).
- **What to expect:** A rapid, painless exam. One or more views are taken
  while you hold your breath for a few seconds.

### Knee X-Ray

- **What it is:** A radiograph of the knee joint, showing the femur, tibia,
  patella, and the joint space between them.
- **What it shows:** Fractures (including subtle hairline fractures); joint
  space narrowing (arthritis); bone alignment and dislocation; calcification
  or loose bodies in the joint.
- **How it works:** X-ray beams pass through the knee from different angles to
  capture multiple 2D views of the bones and joint.
- **Why it's done:** Evaluated for knee pain, swelling, injury, suspected
  fracture, and to monitor joint degeneration.
- **What to expect:** Several views (front, side, angled) are taken. You
  reposition the knee between views; each takes only a few seconds.

### Lumbar Spine X-Ray

- **What it is:** A radiograph of the lower back, showing the lumbar
  vertebrae, sacrum, and the disc spaces between the bones.
- **What it shows:** Vertebral fractures or misalignment; disc space
  narrowing; bone spurs and degeneration (spondylosis); alignment of the
  spinal column (scoliosis, lordosis).
- **How it works:** X-ray beams pass through the lower back to capture front
  and side views of the spine.
- **Why it's done:** Used for persistent low back pain, after trauma, or to
  check spinal alignment and degeneration.
- **What to expect:** Typically front and side views. You may be asked to hold
  still while images are taken.

## MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)

MRI uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves to create detailed
cross-sectional images of soft tissues, organs, and the brain. It does not use
ionizing radiation.

### MRI Brain

- **What it is:** A magnetic resonance scan of the brain that produces
  detailed images of brain tissue, blood vessels, and surrounding structures.
- **What it shows:** Tumors, cysts, and lesions; stroke and bleeding; multiple
  sclerosis plaques; infection and inflammation; white matter changes.
- **How it works:** Strong magnets and radio waves align hydrogen atoms in the
  brain; the signals they emit are used to build detailed cross-sectional
  images. A contrast agent may be injected to highlight blood flow and
  abnormal tissue.
- **Why it's done:** Used for headaches, seizures, suspected stroke, memory
  problems, and to investigate neurological symptoms.
- **What to expect:** You lie still inside a cylindrical scanner for about
  25-45 minutes. The machine makes loud tapping noises; ear protection is
  provided.

### MRI Knee

- **What it is:** A magnetic resonance scan of the knee that clearly images
  soft tissues such as ligaments, tendons, cartilage, and the menisci.
- **What it shows:** Torn menisci or ligaments (e.g., ACL, MCL); cartilage
  damage and arthritis; tendon tears; bone bruising or stress fractures;
  joint fluid (effusion).
- **How it works:** The knee is placed inside a coil and imaged with magnetic
  fields and radio waves to produce detailed soft-tissue images.
- **Why it's done:** After knee injuries, persistent pain or swelling, or when
  an X-ray is not enough to evaluate the soft tissues.
- **What to expect:** Your leg rests in a coil inside the scanner. You must
  remain still for roughly 30 minutes; the procedure is painless.

### MRI Lumbar Spine

- **What it is:** A magnetic resonance scan of the lower back that images the
  vertebrae, spinal cord, nerve roots, and intervertebral discs.
- **What it shows:** Herniated or bulging discs; spinal stenosis (narrowing);
  nerve root compression; degeneration of the discs and joints;
  inflammation or tumors.
- **How it works:** Magnetic fields and radio waves create detailed images of
  the spine and its soft tissues. Contrast may be used to highlight
  inflammation.
- **Why it's done:** For persistent low back pain, radiating leg pain
  (sciatica), numbness, or to evaluate the spinal cord and nerve roots.
- **What to expect:** You lie flat, often with knees slightly bent, for about
  35 minutes. Holding still is important for clear images.

### MRI Abdomen

- **What it is:** A magnetic resonance scan of the abdomen that provides
  detailed images of the liver, kidneys, pancreas, spleen, gallbladder, and
  major blood vessels.
- **What it shows:** Liver lesions, cysts, or tumors; kidney masses or stones;
  pancreatic abnormalities; abscesses or fluid collections; vascular
  conditions (aneurysm, narrowing).
- **How it works:** Magnetic fields and radio waves build detailed images,
  often combined with a contrast agent to evaluate organ blood flow and
  highlight abnormal tissue.
- **Why it's done:** To investigate abdominal pain, masses, abnormal lab
  results, or to further evaluate findings from CT or ultrasound.
- **What to expect:** You may be asked to fast beforehand. You lie on your back
  and hold your breath briefly during image acquisition. The scan time is
  about 50 minutes.
